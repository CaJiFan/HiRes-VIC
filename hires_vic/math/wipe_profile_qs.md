approach: $\approx 50-60%$ of the episode

This means that during the vast majority of surface contact, the agent was operating under the transition phase rather than the wipe phase.

Because transition assigned $K_{\text{trans}} = [10, 100, 10]\text{ N/m}$ and $K_{\text{rot}} = [30, 30, 30]\text{ N}\cdot\text{m/rad}$, the robot spent almost all of its contact time in this ultra-limp mode.

2. Physical Intuition of Stiffness: Which Axis Should Be Stiff?
The physics of contact manipulation and the effect of the $45^\circ$ tilt explain this clearly:

A. Flat Table (Horizontal)
Normal Axis ($Z$): Governs contact force. In impedance control, force is generated by virtual penetration: $F_N = K_z \cdot \Delta z$. To track a target force of $15\text{ N}$ with a realistic commanded penetration of $1.5\text{ cm}$ ($0.015\text{ m}$): $$K_z = \frac{F_N}{\Delta z} = \frac{15\text{ N}}{0.015\text{ m}} = 100\text{ N/m}$$ If $K_z = 10\text{ N/m}$, the force is only $10 \times 0.015 = 0.15\text{ N}$ (virtually zero pressure).
Tangential Axes ($X, Y$): Governs trajectory tracking. Must be stiff ($\approx 120-180\text{ N/m}$) to overcome Coulomb friction $\mu F_N$ and drive the sweeping stroke without sticking.

B. Tilted Table ($45^\circ$ Pitch around Y)

When the table is tilted $45^\circ$ in the $X-Z$ plane:
World-$Y$: Remains purely parallel to the board (horizontal sweep). The LLM made $Y$ stiff because it recognized $Y$ as a purely tangential stroke direction. That part is physically correct.
The $X-Z$ Plane:
The surface normal is $\mathbf{n} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[\hat{\mathbf{x}} + \hat{\mathbf{z}}]$.
The incline tangent (up/down the board) is $\mathbf{t} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}[\hat{\mathbf{x}} - \hat{\mathbf{z}}]$.
If an uncoupled diagonal matrix sets $K_x = 10\text{ N/m}$ and $K_z = 10\text{ N/m}$:

Normal stiffness: $\mathbf{n}^T K \mathbf{n} = \frac{1}{2}(10) + \frac{1}{2}(10) = \mathbf{10\text{ N/m}}$
Incline stiffness: $\mathbf{t}^T K \mathbf{t} = \frac{1}{2}(10) + \frac{1}{2}(10) = \mathbf{10\text{ N/m}}$
Setting $K_x = 10, K_z = 10$ made the arm equally limp along both the contact normal AND along the incline. The robot had no stiffness to generate contact force or to move along the slope.

C. The Physically Correct Distribution:
$K_{\text{trans}}$: Should be $[100, 150, 100]\text{ N/m}$ to $[120, 160, 120]\text{ N/m}$. This provides $\sim 100-120\text{ N/m}$ along the normal for $15\text{ N}$ force tracking and $\sim 150\text{ N/m}$ along the stroke directions.
$K_{\text{rot}}$: Must be high ($\approx 140-180\text{ N}\cdot\text{m/rad}$). A soft $K_{\text{rot}} = 30\text{ N}\cdot\text{m/rad}$ allows the flat wiper pad to twist away from the $+45^\circ$ board orientation upon contact, resulting in edge/corner contact rather than full-pad wiping.
